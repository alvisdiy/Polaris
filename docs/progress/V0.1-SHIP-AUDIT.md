## 2. Phase 2 — the legacy records were not "ambiguous", they were unverifiable

The previous report described 19 approved records as having "ambiguous
provenance". Live verification showed something worse: **every one of those 19
had an `official_page_url` that no longer fetches.**

| Provider | Failure |
|---|---|
| Australia Awards | ReadTimeout |
| Chevening ×3 | ReadTimeout / ReadError |
| CSC | HTTP 412 |
| DAAD ×5 | HTTP 403 |
| Eiffel | HTTP 404 |
| Fulbright | DNS failure |
| LPDP ×3 | HTTP 404 |
| MEXT ×4 | HTTP 404 |

None had ever stored a document. `funding_type` was hand-typed by a seed
script as `"Fully Funded"` with `source_value = NULL` — no recorded source
wording anywhere, for a field that decides whether a student applies.

### 2a. What the sources actually say

Each was re-fetched and the page's own wording read:

| Record | Source wording | Action |
|---|---|---|
| MEXT ×4 | `Tuition \| Exempted`; `Travel allowance \| Round-trip travel expenses (airfare) provided` | kept `Fully Funded` |
| LPDP ×3 | covered components include `Biaya SPP/Tuition Fee`, `Asuransi Kesehatan`, `Biaya Hidup Bulanan` | kept `Fully Funded` |
| Chevening ×3 | `chevening.org/scholarships/`: *"Chevening Scholarships are fully-funded"* | kept `Fully Funded` |
| **DAAD Master Studies** | *"Please note that the DAAD does not cover tuition fees"* | **cleared to NULL** |
| DAAD EPOS | itemised payments, no tuition claim | cleared to NULL |
| DAAD Helmut-Schmidt | no funding statement on the page | cleared to NULL |
| DAAD Research Grants | €1,400/month + allowances, no tuition | cleared to NULL |
| Eiffel | call for applications, no funding statement | cleared to NULL |
| Fulbright | *"Candidates secure their own admission, possible university funding and tuition awards independently"* | cleared to NULL |
| Australia Awards | no funding wording on the page | cleared to NULL |
| **Chinese Government Scholarship** | `csc.edu.cn` and `studyinchina.csc.edu.cn` both HTTP 412 on every connector | **REJECTED** |

One source explicitly contradicted the published value. That is the single
most important finding of the audit: `Fully Funded` was not merely
unsubstantiated on 18 records, it was **false on at least one**, and the
product had been publishing it.

### 2b. The Chevening hand-typed field lists

The three Chevening records had live URLs, so Phase 2B initially left them
alone. Reading their pages properly produced a different kind of finding — a
seed-script subject list contradicted by the source:

* ASEAN page: *"The Chevening ASEAN Scholarship is open to all fields of
  study. There are no specific subject areas prioritised"* — against five
  hand-typed subjects.
* SIDS page: courses must address one of four named development priorities —
  against four hand-typed subjects, three of which the page never mentions.
* Umbrella page: states no subject restriction at all — against six hand-typed
  subjects.

All three lists were replaced through `correct()`, which also gave them the
provenance and version rows they had never had.

## 3. Phase 3 — growth came from the pipeline, not from the seed script

`scripts/v01_phase3_acquire.py` claims pending `opportunity` candidates from
the discovery frontier, fetches them through the existing connectors, runs the
unchanged ingestion pipeline, and approves through `ReviewService.approve`.

**13 new records approved**, all machine-extracted from live pages:
9 PhD studentships and scholarships from `jobs.ac.uk` and
`sheffield.ac.uk`, 1 University of Tokyo programme, plus the USTEP exchange
programme.

### 3a. Nine records the pipeline produced that are not scholarships

Acquisition created nine records that were not scholarships. Seven were caught
by the quality gate (blocked on `degree`, left in review). **Two passed the
gate and were approved**, and had to be withdrawn:

| Record | Source | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `970,000 registrants` | `prospects.ac.uk/postgraduate-recruiters` | a recruitment **advertising sales page**; the extractor took the marketing metric in the middle of its copy as the scholarship's name |
| `PM Yasasvi Post-Matric Scholarship` | `wemakescholars.com/post-doc-scholarships...` | aggregator listing titled *"190+ Post Doc scholarships 2026-27"* |

The other seven were aggregator category pages (`wemakescholars.com`) and a
university calendar page.

This is recorded as a defect, not smoothed over. **The quality gate does not
check that a record's name is a scholarship name**, because that question
needs a judgement about the page's purpose rather than a field comparison.
Phase 3 read a gate pass as sufficient review and was wrong. Anyone re-running
acquisition should expect a human to read the pages, and the aggregator
problem is a live risk (§9).

## 7. Phase 7 — `last_verified_at`

Two defects found and fixed:

1. **`correct()` never set `last_verified_at`**, even when the reviewer passed
   an `evidence_url` — which is exactly the "I checked this against the live
   page" case. Fixed in `services/review/service.py`; a correction *without*
   an evidence URL still does not claim verification.
2. Every record corrected before the fix still showed nothing.

`scripts/v01_backfill_last_verified.py` derives the field from the audit
trail: a review action carrying an `evidence_url` is a moment when someone
attested something against a page, and the most recent such action's
`occurred_at` is when the record was last verified. **42 rows written.**

**Approved coverage: 8/40 → 40/40.** No timestamp was invented; records with
no such action keep NULL.

## 9. Phase 9 — `evidence.page_signals`

The column existed from the frontier's design and **had no writer**. Discovery
recorded why a URL was queued; acquisition recorded what it decided; the two
halves of a candidate's story were never in one place.

Added `FrontierRepository.record_page_signals()` and wired it into
`AcquisitionService` on every fetched page — including the pages it then
refuses to extract, which is where the evidence is most useful.

New: `tests/integration/test_page_signals_recording.py` (6 tests) covering
write, coexistence with observations, replace-not-append semantics, the
no-candidate case (normal, not an error), timestamp shape, and that recording
never changes the candidate's state.

Recording is deliberately best-effort: telemetry must never block ingestion.

## 13. Remaining risks

### 13.1 The listing guard is narrow, on purpose

V0.1.1 closed area 10 with a deterministic guard over page structure, not a
semantic classifier. Stating the shape of what it does and does not cover:

* It answers *"does this page describe one opportunity or many?"* from
  headings, anchors and title. It does **not** answer *"is this definitely a
  scholarship?"* — that question needs a judgement no regex makes, which is
  the whole reason V0.1 shipped two junk records in the first place.
* It detects the failure shapes that were measured. An aggregator that titles
  itself without a count and links to few programmes would read `AMBIGUOUS`
  and proceed to extraction — and then to human review.
* `AMBIGUOUS` is deliberately *not* rejection. Collapsing ambiguity into
  rejection would raise precision and quietly destroy recall on any page the
  heuristics have not seen.
* Measured false-positive rate on the corpus in the database: **0 of the 40
  approved records' own pages are blocked.** That is the number that matters,
  because those pages are the ones already serving users.

### MUST FIX before V0.1.1

1. ~~**The quality gate cannot recognise a non-scholarship.**~~ **CLOSED by
   V0.1.1** — `services/exploration/listing_guard.py`, wired between page
   intent and extraction. Both recorded failures are now blocked before
   extraction; see `docs/progress/V0.1.1-ACQUISITION-GUARD.md`. The residual
   limitation is stated in §13.1: the guard detects the observed shape, not
   every possible one, and `AMBIGUOUS` pages deliberately still reach human
   review.
2. **`degree` is NULL on 6 of 40 approved records** (15%). Four record
   `unmapped_qualification` ("diploma", "foundation programme"), which is the
   honest form of the answer; two record nothing at all. Matching against a
   profile that requires a degree cannot match them. The two with no recorded
   reason are a gap.
3. **20 of 40 approved records have `deadline_state = 'unknown'`** and most of
   those are PhD studentships whose pages state a rolling deadline rather
   than a date. They are visible, but a user cannot tell "open" from "we
   never found out".

Items 2 and 3 remain open and were **not** part of the V0.1.1 scope. They
degrade matching and usefulness; neither publishes anything untrue, and
neither is a blocker.

### ACCEPTABLE for V0.1

4. **Only 20 of 40 have a stored deadline.** The rest are visible and the
   absence is not hidden, but a scholarship without a deadline is of limited
   use.
5. **18 approved records still carry `provenance_origin = 'unknown'`.** Every
   one has now been corrected against a live page with a version row, a
   review action and provenance — the *origin* label is a historical fact
   about how the row was born and is not rewritten.
6. **`source_registry` is mostly untried** (193 of 205 never attempted, 11
   confirmed to hold no scholarship pages). Coverage of the discovery space
   is narrow; the 40 records are a floor, not a survey.
7. **123 frontier URLs are duplicated across countries.** The uniqueness key
   is `(country_id, url_hash)`, so a shared international programme appears
   once per country. Intentional, but it inflates the raw count.
8. **Performance is baselined at 40 records only.** No capacity headroom has
   been established.
9. **`chevening.org` was only reachable through the browser connector.** A
   user-agent change upstream would make three records unverifiable at
   refresh time.

### DEFERRED

10. **Duplicate identity is `provider + name + country`** and a name carrying
    a subtitle escapes it. Unchanged from the previous report and still the
    highest-value correctness fix.
11. **Index-page link following is not in the production path.** Discovery
    refuses to extract an index page but does not follow it. The 561 `done`
    frontier rows include index pages that produced nothing.
12. **172 → 170 inherited Ruff findings.** Deliberate, by decision.

## 14. Decision

**READY_TO_SHIP** — no blockers. (Was `NOT_READY` with one blocker; closed by
V0.1.1.)

**Blocker closed: acquisition can publish a non-scholarship (area 10).** During
this milestone, 9 of 22 records produced by the real pipeline were aggregator
listings or unrelated pages, and 2 of them passed every automated gate and
reached the public API. They were caught by a human reading the documents.

Option (a) below was taken, as this audit recommended. `services/exploration/listing_guard.py`
now runs between page intent and extraction and refuses the two observed
shapes before any `Scholarship` row exists:

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Both recorded failures, replayed from their stored documents | `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING` |
| Both recorded failures, re-fetched live | `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING`, 0 rows created |
| Three legitimate live detail pages (MEXT, Chevening, Honjo) | `NOT_LISTING` — still acquire |
| All 40 approved records' own pages, replayed from stored documents | 0 blocked |
| New tests | 63 (52 unit, 11 integration) |

### How to reach READY_TO_SHIP — as originally written

* **(a)** Add a check that rejects records whose source page is a listing —
   e.g. refuse extraction when the page's dominant heading is a count
   ("190+ Post Doc Scholarships", "62+ Masters Scholarships"), or when
   `page_signals` shows many same-domain links against one detail section.
   Cheap, catches the observed shape, not a general solution. **— taken, as
   V0.1.1.**
* **(b)** Ship with acquisition switched **off** — the 40 curated records
   stand on their own provenance, the pipeline is not required to serve
   users, and the next acquisition run can happen once the check exists.
   Not needed.

### What this decision does not claim

The guard is a deterministic heuristic over page structure, not a semantic
classifier. It closes the defect that was actually observed and measured; it
does not prove that no page can ever produce a non-scholarship. Two
independent things remain true and are stated rather than glossed:

1. **`AMBIGUOUS` pages still extract and still require human review.** The
   guard was built to preserve recall, so a novel junk shape that does not
   match its signals will still reach a reviewer rather than being silently
   dropped. That is a deliberate trade, not an oversight.
2. **Human review remains the backstop**, and it was the backstop that caught
   these two in V0.1. Nothing here removes the need to read new records before
   approving them; it means a reviewer now sees fewer obvious-junk candidates,
   not that the review step is optional.

Two further items are tracked as MUST FIX but do not block: the 6 NULL
`degree` values and the 20 `unknown` deadlines. Both degrade matching and
usefulness; neither publishes anything untrue.
