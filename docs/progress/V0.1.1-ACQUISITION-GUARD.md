# Polaris — V0.1.1 Acquisition Guard

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Branch:** `develop`
**Scope:** close the single V0.1 ship blocker — *acquisition can publish a
non-scholarship* — and nothing else.

This is not a new architecture milestone. No classifier was added to the model
layer, no matching weight moved, no review or publication semantics changed,
and no service layer was introduced. The change is one deterministic boundary
between two existing steps, plus tests.

---

## 1. Problem

The V0.1 ship audit returned `NOT_READY` on exactly one area. From that audit:

> 9 of 22 records produced by the real pipeline were not scholarships.
> 7 were caught by the existing quality gate. **2 passed the automated gate
> and reached the public API.**

| Record | Source page | What the page actually is |
|---|---|---|
| `970,000 registrants` | `prospects.ac.uk/postgraduate-recruiters` | a recruitment **advertising sales page**. The extractor took the marketing metric sitting in the middle of its copy as the scholarship's name. |
| `PM Yasasvi Post-Matric Scholarship` | `wemakescholars.com/post-doc-scholarships-...` | an aggregator **category page** titled *"190+ Post Doc scholarships 2026-27"*, listing 78 programmes. |

Both were caught by a human reading the stored documents, then withdrawn.

---

## 2. Root cause

Forensic inspection of the production path
(`AcquisitionService.acquire_from_source` → fetch → page intent → extraction
→ quality gate → `ReviewService` → approval) is recorded in
`docs/progress/V0.1.1-FORENSIC-FINDING.md`. In short:

**There were two distinct bypasses, not one.**

```
Bypass 1 — the aggregator
  fetch → classify_page_intent → SCHOLARSHIP_DETAIL / 0.75 → EXTRACTED
  Why intent did not stop it: the detail rule counts deadline and eligibility
  *boilerplate* as detail signals, and an aggregator category page carries
  both. Its own page says "Application deadline: 15 March 2027".

Bypass 2 — the marketing page
  fetch → classify_page_intent → UNKNOWN / 0.0 → EXTRACTED
  Why intent did not stop it: acquisition *deliberately* extracts UNKNOWN,
  because UNKNOWN means "not enough evidence either way" rather than "known
  to be a list". That choice is correct for recall and is what carried this
  page through.
```

The quality gate could not catch either, and could not have:

**The gate validates a record's fields. The defect is in the page's purpose.**
A listing page yields a well-formed record — a name, a provider, a country, a
URL, a deadline — because every one of those fields genuinely appears on it.
The name is simply the wrong noun. Asking "is `970,000 registrants` a
scholarship name?" is a judgement about what the page is *for*, and the gate
has no access to that.

---

## 3. Fix

One module, `services/exploration/listing_guard.py`, called from a single site
in `services/exploration/acquisition_service.py` — between page intent and
`run_pipeline`, so a blocked page never reaches extraction and therefore never
becomes a `Scholarship` row.

```
fetch
  ↓
page intent          (unchanged — not weakened)
  ↓
listing guard        (new)
  ↓
extraction           (unchanged)
  ↓
quality gate         (unchanged — not weakened)
  ↓
human review         (unchanged)
  ↓
publication          (unchanged)
```

It is a **tri-state**, per the brief, and only one of the three blocks:

| Verdict | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING` | refuse extraction, no row, reason recorded |
| `NOT_LISTING` | proceed normally |
| `AMBIGUOUS` | **proceed normally**, human review decides |

`AMBIGUOUS` behaving like `NOT_LISTING` is deliberate. Treating uncertainty as
rejection would raise precision and quietly destroy recall on every page the
heuristics have not seen — the same category of error that produced this
blocker.

When the guard refuses a page, the reason goes to the **existing** evidence
channel, not a new telemetry system:

```json
"page_signals": {
  "intent": "SCHOLARSHIP_DETAIL",
  "listing_guard": {
    "reason": "LISTING_PAGE_DETECTED",
    "verdict": "HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING",
    "signals": ["count_heading", "repeated_award_headings", "programme_link_spike"]
  }
}
```

The intent signals are re-recorded alongside the verdict. Recording only one
half would leave a candidate row reading `SCHOLARSHIP_DETAIL` while the
pipeline silently dropped it — invisible to anyone auditing later, and
retryable forever.

## 4. Signals

Four signals, in two families, plus a defence-in-depth reuse of page intent.

**Family A — the page's own claim about itself.** Read from the **title and
first heading only**, never deeper: every university page has a navigation
heading reading "Scholarships", and scanning deeper rejected MEXT — a real,
already-published scholarship.

| Signal | Pattern |
|---|---|
| `count_heading` | a count governing a funding noun: `190+ Post Doc Scholarships`, `62+ Masters Scholarships`, `100 Scholarships`, `50+ Fully Funded Scholarships` |
| `listing_construction_heading` | a plural set construction: `Scholarships 2026-27`, `Scholarships for [country]`, `Scholarships by [category]` |

**Family B — the page's structure is a list.** Never used alone.

| Signal | Threshold | Basis for the threshold |
|---|---|---|
| `repeated_award_headings` | ≥ 8 of the first 40 headings name an award | measured: the aggregator had 78; three would collide with "Benefits"/"Eligibility"/"How to apply" |
| `programme_link_spike` | ≥ 25 funding-shaped links | measured: aggregator 78, university detail page 1 |

**Metric signal — the Prospects shape.** `metric_heading_no_opportunity_claim`
requires *all four* of: a heading that is essentially a figure (`970,000
registants`), zero programme links, no funding claim in the title, zero
funding mentions in the body. Every clause holds for real pages too, so the
conjunction is what makes it safe.

**Defence in depth.** Page intent already refuses discovery surfaces before
extraction; the guard re-asserts it for standalone callers.

Three calibration cases drove these thresholds, all found by measuring the
stored corpus rather than by taste:

* `UNKNOWN` + zero funding mentions is **not** sufficient — USTEP is exactly
  that and is a real published record.
* Singular `Scholarship for Foreign Students` is **not** a listing — Honjo
  International Scholarship Foundation is a real award, and the plural-only
  requirement is load-bearing.
* A large number is not a count — *"Announcing 3 PhD Studentships within
  Royal Holloway"* is one award with three seats, and is published.

## 5. False Positive Protection

`tests/unit/test_listing_guard.py` — 52 tests, all offline. The negative set
covers every case the brief names, plus the three regressions found while
building it:

1. **A scholarship whose title contains a year** — 13 real award titles
   (`MEXT Scholarship for Research Students 2026`, `Einstein Fellowship
   2026`, `Chevening Scholarships 2027`, …).
2. **A scholarship whose title contains a number** — `3 PhD Studentships`,
   `5 Graduate Scholarships`, `Announcing 3 PhD Studentships within Royal
   Holloway`.
3. **A university detail page with many navigation links** — 60 nav links plus
   funding vocabulary; `NOT_LISTING`.
4. **A page containing multiple related links** — three country pages plus
   Apply/Contact; `NOT_LISTING`.
5. **A programme page that is legitimately a single opportunity** — USTEP.
6. **The golden extraction corpus** — `tests/integration/test_golden_extraction.py`
   and the page-intent suites, unchanged and green.

Measured on the whole corpus in the database: **0 of the 40 approved records'
own pages are blocked.** That is the number that matters, because those pages
are the ones already serving users.

The two real-failure fixtures are **generated** from the raw documents V0.1
stored (`scripts/v01_gen_listing_fixtures.py`), not hand-written. Each test
first asserts the page-intent verdict that permitted publication
(`UNKNOWN/0.0` and `SCHOLARSHIP_DETAIL/0.75`) and only then the guard's — so a
hand-written approximation cannot let the suite pass while the guard still
fails on the real page. The generator is idempotent, and a round-trip check
confirms the emitted fixture is byte-identical to the stored document.

| Page | Intent (why it got through) | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| `prospects.ac.uk/postgraduate-recruiters` | `UNKNOWN / 0.0` | `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING` — `metric_heading_no_opportunity_claim` |
| `wemakescholars.com/post-doc-scholarships-...` | `SCHOLARSHIP_DETAIL / 0.75` | `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING` — `count_heading`, `listing_construction_heading`, `repeated_award_headings`, `programme_link_spike` |

The aggregator is additionally blocked with its title reworded, so the verdict
does not rest on a string the publisher can change tomorrow.

### Live level — `scripts/v011_listing_guard_smoke.py`

| URL | Intent | Guard | Expected | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wemakescholars.com/post-doc-...` | DETAIL / 0.75 | `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_LISTING` | BLOCKED | **OK** |

| `hisf.or.jp/en/scholarship/foreigner/` | DETAIL / 0.75 | `NOT_LISTING` | ALLOWED | **OK** |

**Known listings blocked 2/2. Known details allowed 3/3. Scholarship rows
created: 0. Approved before/after: 40 / 40.**

### Pipeline level — `tests/integration/test_acquisition_listing_guard.py`

11 tests through `AcquisitionService.acquire_from_source` with only the HTTP
fetch faked. Asserts the brief's chain literally: detected → no extraction →
no `Scholarship` row → cannot become `APPROVED` → frontier records the reason.
And the converse, so the guard cannot "pass" by blocking everything: a
legitimate detail page extracts, lands in `pending_review`, and a reviewer can
still approve it through `ReviewService`.

Two guards on the tests themselves:

* `test_each_page_reaches_the_fetch_stage` asserts the URL policy admits each
  fixture. A first draft used `/postgraduate-recruiters`, which the URL policy
  filtered — the test passed for the wrong reason. Without that assertion the
  suite would have quietly become a test of the URL policy.
* Rows are scoped by a before/after id snapshot, not by name. The shared
  development database already contains the real `970,000 registrants` record;
  a name-based matcher reports it as damage this run caused.

```
                     baseline of 170; none in any file this milestone touched
```

Warning count varies with test ordering (3–4 observed); all are pre-existing
(a Starlette deprecation, and "coroutine was never awaited" in mocked
discovery tests). None originates in a file this milestone touched.

No existing test was weakened, skipped, or deleted to accommodate the change.

One implementation defect was found and fixed during the work, worth
recording because it would have silently weakened detection:

> `_COUNT_LARGE` originally matched counts with the alternation
> `1[0-9]|[2-9][0-9]`. On `"100 Scholarships"` the first branch matches `10`,
> the mandatory `\s+` then fails against `0`, and the pattern misses
> entirely. `"100 Scholarships"` — a shape the brief names explicitly — was
> **not** being caught. A plain numeric range cannot backtrack into it. The
> threshold is now `[1-9][0-9]{1,5}` with optional thousands separators.

A second: `_METRIC_HEADING` was anchored with `\s*$` and missed the real
stored headings, which carry their full stop inside the heading
(`"970,000 registrants."`).

## 9. Remaining Limitations

**This is a narrow, deterministic heuristic. It is not a universal semantic
classifier, and it does not claim to be.**

* It answers *"does this page describe one opportunity or many?"* from
  headings, anchors and title. It does **not** answer *"is this definitely a
  scholarship?"* — that is the question it was built to stop having to answer,
  but a page that is neither a listing nor an opportunity (a vendor
  advert, a news item with no listing structure) may still extract.
* It detects the shapes that were **measured**. An aggregator that titles
  itself without a count and links to few programmes would read `AMBIGUOUS`,
  proceed to extraction, and land in review — not be blocked.
* `AMBIGUOUS` is deliberately **not** rejection. The trade is explicit: a
  novel junk shape reaches a human rather than being silently dropped.
* The guard runs per page. A single detail page reached via an aggregator's own
  listing link is judged on its own content, which is the intended behaviour
  but means the aggregator's brand is not itself a signal.
* Thresholds (8 award headings, 25 links) are calibrated on 395 stored
  documents, not on the wider web. An aggregator with a quieter page would sit
  below them.

**Human review remains the backstop.** It was the backstop that caught these
two records in V0.1. A reviewer now sees fewer obvious-junk candidates; the
review step itself is unchanged and still required.

## 10. Ship Decision

```
READY_TO_SHIP
```

The V0.1 audit's single blocker — *acquisition can publish a non-scholarship* —
is closed with deterministic page evidence at the boundary the audit
recommended, and verified offline against the two pages that actually failed
and live against those same pages today.

`docs/progress/V0.1-SHIP-AUDIT.md` is updated accordingly: **Area 10 FAIL →
PASS**, decision **`NOT_READY` → `READY_TO_SHIP`**, 0 blockers. The audit's
criteria were not modified — only the verdict and its evidence, and a new
§13.1 recording what the guard does not cover.

The two open MUST FIX items (6 NULL `degree` values, 20 `unknown` deadlines)
were out of scope, are unchanged, and are not blockers: they degrade matching
and usefulness without publishing anything untrue.

**Caveat on scope:** this decision rests on the guard closing the *observed*
failure. Anyone enabling acquisition against a substantially different source
mix should re-run the corpus measurement first — it is one command, and the
generator that produced the fixtures will re-derive them against new
documents.
